## L3 should be a numerical service safeguard

Suburban: Case 5.5 corporate accounts × 3 orders/week = 16.5 orders/week,
12 cups/order = 198 cups/week. With 2 windows/day and 5 weekdays/week,
**19.8 cups/window**, rising to **26.6 cups/window** under the
illustrative landlord account uplift.

Pilot gates: keep walk-in service at/below **3.5 min**, satisfaction
at/above **4.2/5**, track corporate on-time deliveries, confirm that
2 production windows can handle the *peak* load. The average above
does **not** prove operational capacity. Production slots create
**$0 directly booked incremental NPV** when the Case assumes all orders
are fulfilled. Do not add fictitious extra café sales.

Optional downside sensitivity (NOT a source fact): if the absence
of fixed slots would cause 5% loss of otherwise captured corporate
orders, L3 could protect about **$71.7k** HQ five-year NPV in the
all-levers implementation. This is a *risk-at-value* illustration,
not promised new cash flow. Keep off the main NPV bridge or
put in a small sensitivity footnote.
